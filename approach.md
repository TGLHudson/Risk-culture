Hudson Risk Culture (HRC) starts from one position: the culture an organisation needs is set by the risks it runs, not by a ranking of maturity.

## Fit, not maturity

Most safety and risk culture models place an organisation on a scale and recommend that it climb. That treats culture as a score to improve in the abstract. We ask a different question: given the hazards, the operations and the uncertainty this organisation faces, what culture does it need, and does the culture it has fit?

## Where the ladder comes from

The safety culture ladder originates with Dr P. T. W. Hudson. He is the father of T. G. L. Hudson, the author of this work, and we state that relationship openly. *The Culture Ladder* (CRC Press, February 2027) builds on his ladder and reframes it around fit to risk. See [The Culture Ladder]({{ '/culture-ladder/' | relative_url }}).

## Working against the risk

We are not working for management; we are working against the risk. An engagement succeeds when management and workforce agree on how the risks being run are managed. That makes the work pro-worker and pro-union: the people closest to the hazard are part of the agreement, not its subject.
